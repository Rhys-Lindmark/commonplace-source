## Conservative extraction recommendations

The JSON includes `extractionGuards` and small real-corpus `extractionExamples`. They are implementation guidance, not assertions that every example's date has been independently verified.

1. Inspect `provenance.citations` as well as source/descriptions. The current `audit_dates.classify()` ignores that field, leaving clear citation dates unused.
2. Normalize punctuation-only suffixes such as `January 23, 2019 (;)`. Preserve substantive parentheses and competing edition dates.
3. Identify a clearly attached primary event date before a later carrier. A letter dated 9 November 1869 reprinted in a 1917 collection should retain both typed dates, not arbitrarily select 1917 or whichever number is smallest.
4. Do not infer publication from historical subjects in a title. Book/page/Bekker identifiers such as `1103b` are not years. Ebook release/update years, author/editor lifetimes, and narrated historical years are separate.
5. Split primary-event evidence from a later “quoted in” clause where unambiguous; do not discard a valid earlier letter/speech date solely because the remainder mentions a reprint. Conversely, an entire paragraph by a later commentator may provide only a carrier date. Example: a Margaret Fuller quote discussed by Perry Miller in 1957 does not establish that Fuller spoke in 1957.
6. Preserve conflict and attribution uncertainty internally. Estimate after source checks when no suitable date survives; an author-lifespan formula is an estimate, not evidence of a verified quotation date. Check person identity/type and name punctuation aliases before using birth/death data.

Do not automatically date the expanded Einstein record `z6ZCI63jw-9k` to 1929: its additional explanatory sentence is absent from the checked interview passage. Nor should King’s 1968 speech date be assigned to Charles A. Beard's similar stars wording. These explicit exclusions are in the JSON.
